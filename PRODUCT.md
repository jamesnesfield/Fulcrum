## Users

Primarily the author: a photographer editing their own work, often straight off a camera card, and judging tone and colour at the desk. The tool is personal first, and may be shared with or released to other photographers later. Who those photographers would be has not been decided.

## Product Purpose

Fulcrum is an opinionated photo editor. It takes a shoot from the card to finished, uploaded photos (import and cull, edit, export or upload) in one lean app. It is built around a specific way of editing, not a general-purpose toolbox. It succeeds when a photo reaches the look the author wants in fewer, more deliberate moves than a general editor needs.

## Positioning

Fulcrum is opinionated photo editing. Its tools encode one editing approach instead of offering every option. The clearest example is the tonal model: global contrast, masked local contrast and B&W tone all pivot on the image's measured median, so a curve stays locked where the photo's tones actually sit. That is where the app's name comes from.

## Operating Context

- **Import:** connect a camera or card (ImageCaptureCore). Each shot is a RAW+JPEG pair treated as one unit, flagged keep or reject, then imported.
- **Edit:** a file list sidebar, the photo canvas, and an inspector holding the light, contrast, colour mixer and grading, B&W, local mask and detail panels. Before/after comes from holding Space or pressing `\`, split view from Y, crop from C.
- **Reuse:** copy and paste settings between photos, and save Looks. A Look holds every setting except the crop, and its masks re-measure on each photo.
- **Deliver:** export, or upload to Apple Photos (with keywords and favourites) or to Google Photos through the user's own OAuth client.
- **Reflect:** every edit is appended to a JSON-lines edit log, and the Style Patterns window summarises habits across photos.
- `fulcrum-check` is an engine test harness that asserts pipeline behaviour on synthesised images.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Supports camera RAW (CIRAWFilter), JPEG, HEIC/HEIF, PNG and TIFF. Fulcrum registers as an alternate editor for these types.
- Processing uses Core Image. The pipeline order is fixed: decode/WB → exposure → highlights/shadows → measure median → median-pivot contrast → local adjustments → clarity → vibrance/saturation.
- Local masks: Sky (auto), Subject (auto), Linear Gradient, Luminance Range, Whole Image.
- Google Photos API limits (since 2025): upload, create albums and add Fulcrum's own uploads to them. There is no favourites or keywords API.
- Built with SwiftPM and `scripts/build-app.sh`, ad-hoc signed. It is not notarised or distributed yet.
- **Undecided:** distribution, pricing and audience beyond the author.

## Brand Commitments

- The name is Fulcrum, after the pivot that the tonal model turns on.
- The app icon is generated by `scripts/make-icon.swift`.
- Copy is plain, precise and British-English ("favourite", "colour"), and it explains mechanisms directly, e.g. "Masks like Sky re-measure their median on each photo."

## Product Principles

1. **The photo is the hero.** Chrome stays minimal and colour-neutral so it never sways the user's judgement of the image.
2. **Native macOS, not a skin.** Use standard SwiftUI/AppKit controls, menus, inspectors and sheets, and behave the way a Mac app is expected to.
3. **Keyboard-first.** Single-key shortcuts (C, Y, `\`, Space, `.`) and menu equivalents are part of the product, and they must remain.
4. **Opinionated over exhaustive.** Add a control only if it serves Fulcrum's way of editing. Fewer, more meaningful controls beat feature parity.
5. **Explain the mechanism.** When a tool does something clever, such as re-measuring a median or pairing RAW+JPEG, the UI says so plainly.
